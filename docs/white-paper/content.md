# MANIFESTO

### There's power in communal knowledge.

As queer people navigating the world, a lot of us can experience instances of awkwardness, discrimination, and even violence directed towards us.

Most of these experiences go unreported, and this knowledge never makes it to other queer people, who could use it to avoid unsafe spaces.

We want to be able to warn each other about people and places to avoid by people like us.

And even if not unsafe for us, we want to avoid supporting the businesses of queerphobic people.

On the flip side, there are wonderful allies out there who do all they can to make us feel safe and comfortable in their spaces.

If we could point each other to those places, we could have more opportunities to feel safe and free as openly queer people.

Also, we want to support and promote the businesses of allies that make our lives better.

And at the same time, we can use our platform to support and promote queer-owned spaces, local businesses, valuable initiatives, etc.

### There's power in specific knowledge.

Different queer people feel safe in different places. We believe that saying a place is "LGBTQ+ friendly" means little to nothing, especially for people with intersectional characteristics.

Beyond just safety, we can use our platform to share location-based information that's relevant to subsets of the queer population.

Some examples: places with unisex toilets, important for people that will feel uncomfortable or unsafe in gendered toilets; places more or less suited for people with different disabilities; medical specialists with specific knowledge and sensibility for different bodies and conditions; etc.

### There's power in real-life communities.

We all know that we're more "connected" yet isolated than ever. And we're all aware of the individual and societal consequences that this brings. Mental health issues, viral right-wing ideologies, etc.

As part of an oppressed minority, it is even more important for us to foster local, real-life connections and networks.

We want to create software that helps people spend less time hooked on their phones. Not more.

We want to create software that helps people spend more joyful time with people, not less.

We want to empower real-life communities by helping them organize and share knowledge that's valuable to them.

We want people to know that their contributions are valuable and they won't be lost even if we can't continue building the app at some point.

### There's power in technology.

We want to take as much of this power away from big tech as we can.

We're not interested in making technology addictive.

We're not interested in mindlessly shoving AI into technology.

We want to create an app that feels unique and special, yet widely accessible.

We want the map to feel dynamic, alive, and interactive.

We prefer open source software and open protocols.

We want users to own their data. They should be able to export it and access it via alternative methods to our app.

We want others to be able to create their own clients, so that the users aren't beholden to our leadership if they disagree with us at any point.

## How

### A specific map made by specific queer people

This is not a map of queer businesses, a guide of queer events or a social map app, although such data and features may become part of it.

It's an open-source, queer, collaborative map.

It is built by a subset of people of Colectivo Trueno, which is a group of people with strong opinions about why and how technology can be used to empower queer communities.

Our backgrounds, visions and morals inform the development of this app.

There are several maps of queer places, and that's totally cool and useful. We also have queer and queer-owned places in our map, but that's not the interesting or unique part about this project, because anybody can search for "gay bar" or "LGBTIQ+ library" on Google Maps, and that's fine.

The interesting part is non-queer places. Whether we recommend them or not to other people.

### A map made of collaborative layers

#### The queer safety layer

This is the first layer that we're working on, and the defining feature of Proyecto Mapa in its initial phase.

#### Other layers by us

#### Community layers

In our platform, communities can collaborate and share location-based information in specific data layers that they own and manage.

Community layers can be public or private. They can have different rules for access and collaboration. They can have different visual identities, types of places, ways to rate places, ways to react to contributions.

### Open protocol

We want to create a data protocol for the collaborative data created in the map.

#### Potential monetization

The protocol is to be open and non-profit forever. The app that uses the protocol can be monetized if we find a way to do it that's respectful of users and allows us to make the project sustainable long term.
